# Competitive Landscape, Go-to-Market, and Build Economics for US Outpatient (Professional-Fee) Pre-Bill AI Chart Review and AI Coding

Research date: 2026-10-21. US only. "Vendor claim" marks figures that come from a company's own materials or press releases and have not been independently verified. "Aggregator" marks third-party profile sites (Tracxn, CB Insights, YesPress, RevCycleAI, etc.) whose numbers often conflict.

## Q1. Who would a new entrant compete with? (Competitors 2024–2026: scope, segment, funding, customers, pricing)

### Takeaway
The well-funded "autonomous coding" vendors (Nym, Fathom, CodaMetrix, AKASA, SmarterDx, Solventum, Optum) mostly sell to **hospitals and large health systems**, and their autonomous coding is concentrated in **radiology and emergency departments**. Charta Health is the clearest **outpatient, pre-bill, "review 100% of charts"** player, aimed at primary care, urgent care and behavioral health groups, FQHCs and MSOs. The biggest new threat is the **EHR vendors building coding in**: Epic's Penny is live for professional coding at 360+ organizations, and athenahealth launched Express Coding in summer 2026. Ambient-scribe companies (Abridge, Ambience, Suki, Microsoft Dragon Copilot, Commure) are also adding coding suggestions at the point of care.

### Plain-English terms (for a non-healthcare reader)
- **Outpatient / ambulatory**: care where the patient goes home the same day (clinic visits, urgent care, telehealth). **Inpatient**: hospital stays.
- **Professional fee ("pro-fee")**: the clinician's own bill for their work, separate from the hospital's "facility" bill. Most physician-practice billing is pro-fee.
- **Coding**: turning a clinician's note into standard billing codes. **CPT** codes say what was done, and **ICD-10** codes say why (the diagnosis). **E/M** ("evaluation and management") codes are the office-visit codes, set at levels 2–5 (e.g., 99213 vs 99214); higher levels pay more. **Modifiers** (e.g., "-25") are suffixes that change how a code is paid.
- **Pre-bill review**: checking the chart and the codes *before* the claim goes to the insurer (the "payer"), rather than auditing after payment.
- **Autonomous coding**: AI assigns the codes and sends the claim with no human touch when it is confident. **CAC (computer-assisted coding)**: AI suggests and a human coder approves. **Audit**: checking codes someone else already chose.
- **CDI (clinical documentation integrity)**: making sure the note actually supports the codes, and asking doctors to clarify when it doesn't.
- **RVU**: Medicare's unit of physician work, which drives payment. **Denial**: the payer refuses the claim. **Downcoding**: the payer pays a lower level than billed. **Upcoding**: billing a higher level than documentation supports, which is a fraud and compliance risk.
- **HCC / risk adjustment**: diagnosis codes that raise payments in Medicare Advantage and other value-based contracts.
- **RCM (revenue cycle management)**: the whole billing-and-collections process; "RCM companies" are outsourced billing firms. **MSO**: a management services organization that runs back-office work for many physician practices, often private-equity-backed. **FQHC**: a federally qualified health center (community clinic).
- **KLAS**: an independent healthcare-IT research firm that surveys customers. **Epic Toolbox**: Epic's designation for third-party products that follow its recommended integration.

### Inferences
- **Threat ranking for an outpatient pre-bill entrant:**
  1. **EHR-native coding**. Epic Penny, athena Express Coding and Experity/Exdion directly target the pro-fee coding job, inside the workflow, and are likely bundled.
  2. **Charta, RapidClaims, CombineHealth, Ember and other ambulatory-focused startups**. These sell to the same small and mid-sized groups.
  3. **Ambient scribes**. They code at the point of care, before the bill exists, and could make "pre-bill" review partly redundant for their customers. They are currently stronger in health systems.
  4. **Enterprise autonomous coders** (Nym, Fathom, CodaMetrix, AKASA, Solventum, Optum). These focus on health systems and ED/radiology and rarely sell to a 20-provider primary-care group.
- Scribe coding is "at creation" and mostly for the clinician. A **pre-bill review layer that is independent of the documentation vendor** ("second set of eyes") stays defensible because payers and compliance officers value independence. A scribe grading its own notes is a conflict of interest. This is an inference, not a sourced claim.
- Epic's autonomous coding started in ED and radiology, the same beachhead as Nym, Fathom and CodaMetrix. That compresses that segment for startups. Outpatient primary care, behavioral health, urgent care and specialty clinics on non-Epic EHRs are less contested by EHR-native tools today, except athena and Experity customers.

## Q2. Where is the whitespace? (Underserved segments and underserved jobs)

### Takeaway
The clearest whitespace is (a) **small and mid-sized independent and MSO-owned outpatient groups on non-Epic EHRs** where physicians pick their own codes and nobody reviews them; (b) specialties the enterprise vendors haven't reached (behavioral health, dermatology, orthopedics, OB/GYN, home health, FQHCs); and (c) jobs beyond "assign the code." These jobs include **two-way (bi-directional) compliance**, meaning catching both under-coding (lost revenue) and over-coding (audit risk), plus **payer-specific downcoding defense** (e.g., Cigna's 2025 E/M policy) and **provider education/feedback**.

### Cited Findings
- Most provider groups review only ~1–2% of charts; Charta's pitch is 100% pre-bill review. — [Madrona](https://www.madrona.com/charting-healthcares-ai-transformation-investing-in-charta-health/); [BCV](https://baincapitalventures.com/insight/charting-a-new-era-how-charta-health-is-transforming-medical-chart-review/)
- Charta's claimed 15.2% RVU uplift is specifically "in organizations with physician-led coding," i.e., practices where doctors choose their own codes. — [Charta](https://www.chartahealth.com/resources/series-a)
- athenahealth publishes content on the "burden of clinician-led coding" in its ambulatory base. — [athenahealth blog](https://www.athenahealth.com/resources/blog/managing-burdens-of-clinician-led-medical-coding)
- Autonomous coding adoption is concentrated in radiology and ED, and customers "want to broaden use cases." Accuracy is inconsistent in certain specialties. — [KLAS](https://engage.klasresearch.com/blog/from-hype-to-reality-what-healthcare-leaders-should-know-about-autonomous-coding-solutions/8341/)
- The enterprise vendors' stated beachheads are ED and radiology; Nym names outpatient surgery and specialty clinics only as later expansion. — [Nym](https://blog.nym.health/what-to-know-autonomous-medical-coding); [Healthcare IT Today 2026](https://www.healthcareittoday.com/2026/08/19/epic-ugm-2026-judy-faulkner-keynote-and-cool-stuff-ahead/)

- An older OIG study (2010 data) found 42% of E/M claims incorrectly coded, projecting $6.7B in overpayments. — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/oig-improper-evaluation-management-payments-cost-medicare-6-7b.html)

- **Outpatient AI adoption lags.** Menlo/Morning Consult found AI adoption of 27% among providers overall but 18% among outpatient organizations (Aug–Sep 2025 survey of 700+ executives). — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-ai-in-healthcare/)
- **Provider feedback/education** is already a Charta product line ("provider feedback"). Auditors' standard output is per-provider findings plus documentation improvement and training recommendations. — [Charta](https://www.chartahealth.com/resources/series-a); [AAPC](https://www.aapc.com/codes/coding-newsletters/my-internal-medicine-coding-alert/protect-compliance-educate-physicians-increase-efficiency-and-detect-lost-revenue-perform-internal-coding-audits-article)

- **Payer side as an adjacent market.** Charta sells risk-adjustment and RADV products to payers, and Fathom now counts payers among its adopters. — [Charta](https://www.chartahealth.com/resources/series-a); [Fathom/CVS](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260504086674/en/Fathom-Secures-Strategic-Investment-From-CVS-Health-Ventures-to-Accelerate-Enterprise-Wide-Autonomous-Coding)

### Inferences
- **Best-fit wedge segments for a new entrant:**
  - (1) MSO/PE-backed multi-site outpatient groups (one contract, many practices, central RCM leader). Charta lists MSOs as a target.
  - (2) FQHCs and behavioral health, which are high-volume, low-reimbursement, with physician-led coding and thin RCM staff.
  - (3) Specialty groups not yet covered by enterprise autonomous coders, e.g., dermatology, orthopedics, GI, OB/GYN, pain, and PT/OT.
  - (4) Practices on eCW, NextGen, ModMed or Greenway, where no native AI coding product was found (versus Epic and athena, which now have native tools).
- **Best-fit wedge jobs:**
  - (1) "Defend against downcoding": prove each level-4/5 E/M is supported before submission, and assemble Cigna-style exemption evidence packets.
  - (2) Bi-directional compliance reports for compliance officers and boards, flagging both under- and over-coding, which helps an independent vendor avoid looking like an "upcoding engine."
  - (3) Per-provider education loops (monthly scorecards), which create stickiness and justify per-provider pricing.
  - (4) Payer-contract-specific rules (modifier 25, NCCI, telehealth POS, behavioral health add-on codes).
- Charta has already moved toward (1)–(4) and toward payers, so a new entrant should pick a narrower vertical (one specialty plus one or two EHRs) to differentiate, rather than going horizontal against Charta.

- No independent data on how much revenue outpatient groups lose to downcoding by Aetna, UHC or others beyond the Cigna policy.

## Q4. What does it take to build an MVP and reach first paying customers? (Team, time, money, margins)

### Inferences
- **Illustrative per-chart cost model** (my assumptions; not sourced). Assume ~15K input tokens (note, prior visits, payer rules excerpt), ~2K output tokens, and 2–3 model passes on Sonnet 5.5. That costs ≈ $0.03 + $0.02 = ~$0.05 per pass, or ~$0.10–$0.15 per chart, and roughly half that via the Batch API, which suits overnight pre-bill review. Caching shared guideline text cuts it further. The newer tokenizer produces ~30% more tokens, per Anthropic's page. **LLM cost is on the order of cents per chart.**
- **Human QA dominates COGS.** At ~$64.5K salary × ~1.3 benefits load ÷ (45 charts/day × ~230 days), a full onshore CPC review costs roughly **$8 per chart**. QA on 5% of charts adds ~$0.40/chart, and 20% adds ~$1.60/chart. Offshore QA at $18–45/hr cuts this substantially. So the MVP's margin depends on (a) how fast the QA sampling rate can drop as accuracy is proven, and (b) whether coders are only reviewing flagged charts (exception-based).
- **Realistic MVP team** (based on comparables):
  - 2 technical founders, ideally with at least one obtaining a CPC (~3–6 months of study at minimum; Charta's founders took ~1 year including discovery).
  - 1–2 contract or part-time certified coders/auditors (CPC/CRC) to label gold-standard charts and QA output.
  - A physician advisor or CMO for credibility.
  - Once pilots start, 1 forward-deployed engineer per 3–5 early customers to handle EHR exports, PDFs and scanned documents, and specialty rules.
- **Realistic timeline**: 3–6 months to a usable retrospective-analysis product on exported charts (no deep EHR integration); first paid pilots 1–3 months after outreach to outpatient groups (consistent with Charta's 60 days and Menlo's 4.7-month average); 6–12 months to production integration with one or two EHRs.
- **Money**: Charta reached revenue before raising a seed, so a pre-seed of ~$0.5–2M (founder runway + coders + SOC 2/HIPAA + cloud) is plausible. The category's seed range is ~$1.2M–$8.1M (Maia → Charta). This is an inference from the comparables listed, not a benchmark study.
- **Compliance prerequisites** that gate first revenue: BAA, HIPAA security program, likely SOC 2 Type I/II (Maverick advertises SOC 2), plus a PHI de-identification and retention policy. These are commonly demanded by MSOs and health systems (inference; no source directly measured).
